What you're up against

Legacy uses standard, battle-tested primitives straight from the browser's Web Crypto API.
Full details are in the protocol spec.

1

Two keys, both required

The seed was encrypted under a benefactor key and a beneficiary key, joined into a single password. You need both, exactly, to decrypt.

2

600,000 rounds of key stretching

The password goes through PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations and a random 16-byte salt. Every single guess costs an attacker 600,000 hashes, and the salt makes precomputed tables useless.

3

AES-256-GCM

The seed is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, the same authenticated cipher that protects most of the world's HTTPS traffic. The ciphertext carries no header, no marker, and no fixed bytes — every visible character is random.

4

Strong, randomly chosen keys

The keys for this challenge were generated the way our Security guide recommends: multiple independent random words, no personal meaning, no relationship between the two. Profiling us won't help. That's the point: Legacy is as strong as the keys you choose, and these were chosen well.

Rules

  • No sign-up, no entry fee, no purchase. Anyone can try. The payload is public.
  • Winner takes it by sweeping it. If you decrypt the seed, the wallet is yours. Move the coins and you've won. No claim form, no permission needed.
  • Wallet details: standard BIP-84 native SegWit (m/84'/0'/0'), no BIP-39 passphrase. Your decrypted seed opens it directly in any wallet.
  • Attack the cryptography, not people. Phishing, social engineering, or breaking into anyone's devices or accounts is out of bounds — and illegal. This is a test of the protocol and the ciphertext only.
  • Tell us how you did it. If you win, we'd love a write-up. A real flaw would get fixed and you'd get full credit (and bragging rights forever).
  • No end date. The challenge runs until the coins move. We may add to the bounty over time; we will never remove it while the challenge is open.
